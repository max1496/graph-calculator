Math Basics

In short: The core ideas from upper elementary to middle school, in short pieces with an interactive slope graph.

This page collects the core ideas from upper elementary school through middle school. Each one is short, with a picture or an example. Once these feel easy, Algebra 1 is much smoother.

1. Slope and y-intercept (try it)

Move the sliders and watch the line.

  • y = mx + b is called slope-intercept form.
  • Slope = rise ÷ run = how much y changes when x goes up by 1.
  • m > 0: the line goes up. m < 0: goes down. m = 0: flat.
  • y-intercept = the point (0, b).
  • Slope from two points: m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁).

2. The coordinate plane

  • Two number lines cross at the origin (0, 0). The horizontal one is the x-axis, the vertical one is the y-axis.
  • A point is written (x, y): go over first, then up or down.
  • The four sections are called quadrants. (+,+) is I, (−,+) is II, (−,−) is III, (+,−) is IV.

3. Integers and the number line

  • Bigger numbers are further right. −2 is bigger than −5.
  • Subtracting a negative = adding: 5 − (−3) = 8.
  • Same signs multiply to positive. Different signs multiply to negative.
  • Absolute value |−4| = 4 is the distance from 0.

  • Percent of a number: change the percent to a decimal and multiply. 25% of 80 = 0.25 × 80 = 20.
  • To add fractions, make the bottoms the same first. 1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

6. Ratios and proportions

  • A ratio compares two amounts: 2 cups of flour for 3 servings is 2 : 3.
  • A proportion says two ratios are equal: 2/3 = x/12.
  • Cross-multiply: 3x = 24, so x = 8.
  • Unit rate = per one: $6 for 3 apples is $2 per apple.

7. Solving equations: keep the balance

  • An equation is a balance scale. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other side.
  • Undo operations in reverse order.
Example: 3x + 5 = 20
Subtract 5 from both sides: 3x = 15
Divide both sides by 3: x = 5
Check: 3(5) + 5 = 20 ✓
