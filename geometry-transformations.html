Geometry Transformations

In short: Enter the corners of a shape, choose a transformation, and see where every corner goes. The blue shape is the original and the red shape is the image.

The four transformations

  • Translation: every point slides the same distance in the same direction. (x, y) becomes (x + dx, y + dy).
  • Reflection: every point flips over a line, the mirror line. Reflecting over the x-axis gives (x, −y). Over the y-axis it gives (−x, y). Over the line y = x it swaps the coordinates to (y, x).
  • Rotation: every point turns around a center by the same angle. About the origin, 90° counterclockwise gives (−y, x), 180° gives (−x, −y) and 270° gives (y, −x).
  • Dilation: every point moves away from or toward a center. About the origin, (x, y) becomes (kx, ky). If |k| > 1 the shape gets bigger, and if 0 < |k| < 1 it gets smaller.

Congruent or similar?

Translations, reflections and rotations are rigid motions. They keep side lengths and angles the same, so the image is congruent to the original. A dilation keeps the angles but multiplies every length by |k|, so the image is similar to the original, not congruent unless |k| = 1.
