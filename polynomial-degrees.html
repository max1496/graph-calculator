Polynomials by Degree

In short: A degree n polynomial has at most n real roots and n − 1 turning points. Odd degrees always cross the x-axis.

The big picture

For a polynomial of degree n with real coefficients:

  • At most n real roots (counting multiplicity). Exactly n complex roots.
  • At most n − 1 turning points (peaks and valleys).
  • At most n − 2 inflection points (where concavity changes).
  • Non-real roots come in conjugate pairs, so the number of real roots has the same parity as n.
  • Odd degree: at least 1 real root. Ends go opposite ways.
  • Even degree: may have no real roots. Ends go the same way.

Degree 3 (cubic) in detail
  • Always crosses the x-axis at least once.
  • Has exactly one inflection point, and the graph is symmetric about it.
  • Either 2 turning points or none (like x³).
  • Formula exists for the roots, but the factor theorem and the Rational Root Theorem are usually faster.
Degree 4 (quartic) in detail
  • Looks like a “W” (3 turns) or a “U” (1 turn).
  • Often factors like a quadratic in x²: x⁴ − 5x² + 4 = (x² − 1)(x² − 4).
  • Even function if only even powers appear.
Degree 5 and higher
  • Degree 5 and up: no general formula using roots and arithmetic (Abel–Ruffini). We use graphs and numeric methods.
  • Odd degree always has a real root, which the Intermediate Value Theorem guarantees.

Roots and multiplicity

  • A factor (x − r)m gives a root r with multiplicity m.
  • Odd multiplicity: the graph crosses the axis.
  • Even multiplicity: the graph touches the axis and turns back.
  • Multiplicity 1 crosses straight, 3 crosses with a flat wiggle.

Practice

  1. A cubic has how many real roots at least?
    Show answer

    At least 1. Complex roots come in pairs, so 3 − 2 = 1 is the minimum.

  2. What is the most turning points a degree 6 polynomial can have?
    Show answer

    5 (that is n − 1).

  3. f(x) = −x⁴ + 3x. Describe the end behavior.
    Show answer

    Even degree, negative leading coefficient: down on both sides.

  4. f(x) = (x − 1)²(x + 2). What happens at each root?
    Show answer

    At x = 1 (multiplicity 2) the graph touches and turns. At x = −2 (multiplicity 1) it crosses.
