Function Transformations

In short: Pick a function, then shift, stretch, flip or rotate it. The dashed gray graph is the original and the blue graph is the new one.

How each change works

  • Shift up or down (k): y = f(x) + k moves every point up by k. A negative k moves it down.
  • Shift right or left (h): y = f(x − h) moves every point right by h. This one feels backward: subtracting moves the graph to the right.
  • Vertical stretch (a): y = a · f(x) multiplies every height by a. If a is between 0 and 1 the graph is squashed.
  • Horizontal squeeze (b): y = f(bx) squeezes the graph toward the y-axis when b is greater than 1 and stretches it when b is between 0 and 1.
  • Reflection: y = −f(x) flips the graph over the x-axis. y = f(−x) flips it over the y-axis.
  • Rotation: turning the graph around the origin by an angle moves each point (x, y) to (x cos θ − y sin θ, x sin θ + y cos θ). A rotated graph is often no longer a function, so it has no formula of the form y = f(x).

Order of the changes

The tool applies the changes in this order: squeeze and reflect over the y-axis inside the function, then the shift h, then the stretch a and reflection over the x-axis, then the shift k, and finally the rotation. Changing the order can give a different graph, which is why teachers ask you to do them in a fixed order.
