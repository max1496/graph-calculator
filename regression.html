What is linear regression?

Linear regression finds the straight line y = ax + b that best fits a set of points. Best means the line that makes the sum of the squared vertical distances from the points to the line as small as possible, which is called the least squares method.

Formulas

  • Slope: a = Σ(x − x̄)(y − ȳ) / Σ(x − x̄)2
  • Intercept: b = ȳ − ax̄
  • Correlation: r = Σ(x − x̄)(y − ȳ) / √[Σ(x − x̄)2 · Σ(y − ȳ)2]
  • Standard error of the estimate: √[Σ(y − ŷ)2 / (n − 2)]

How to read the results

  • r is between −1 and 1. Its sign shows whether y rises or falls with x, and values near ±1 mean the points sit close to a line.
  • R² is r squared. It is the share of the variation in y that the line explains, so 0.9 means about 90%.
  • Standard error of the estimate is the typical distance between the points and the line, in the units of y. It needs at least 3 points.
  • A high R² does not prove that x causes y, and a handful of points can be misleading. Always check the scatter plot for outliers.

Sample or population standard deviation?

Use the sample version (divide by n − 1) when your points are a sample from a larger group, which is the usual case. Use the population version (divide by n) only when your points are every value that exists.

Good to know

  • You need at least 2 points. With exactly 2 points the line passes through both, R² is 1, and the standard error is undefined.
  • If every x value is the same, the points form a vertical line and there is no slope.
