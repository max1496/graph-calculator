Continuity and Derivatives

In short: A continuous graph has no breaks. The derivative is the tangent slope, and corners, cusps and breaks stop it from existing.

Continuity

  • A function is continuous if you can draw it without lifting your pencil.
  • Precisely, f is continuous at x = a when all three are true:
    1. f(a) exists.
    2. The limit of f(x) as x → a exists.
    3. The limit equals f(a).
  • Polynomials, sin, cos and eˣ are continuous everywhere. 1/x is continuous everywhere it is defined.

Kinds of discontinuity

Hole (removable)The limit exists, but f(a) is missing or different.(x² − 1)/(x − 1) at x = 1

Infinite (asymptote)The function goes to ±∞.1/x at x = 0

The Intermediate Value Theorem needs a continuous function. See the IVT page.

The derivative

  • The derivative f′(a) is the slope of the tangent line at x = a.
  • It is the instantaneous rate of change, like the speedometer reading at one moment.
  • Definition: f′(a) = limh→0 (f(a+h) − f(a)) / h
  • f′ > 0: the graph is going up. f′ < 0: going down. f′ = 0: flat (a possible max or min).
  • Power rule: (xn)′ = nxn−1. So (x²)′ = 2x.

In the calculator, tap Tangent and tap a curve to draw the tangent line and see its slope.

When is a function NOT differentiable?

  • Corner: the slope jumps. |x| at 0.
  • Cusp: slopes go to opposite infinities. x2/3 at 0.
  • Vertical tangent: the slope is infinite. ∛x at 0.
  • Discontinuity: a hole, jump or asymptote.
Differentiable ⇒ continuous. But not the other way around: |x| is continuous at 0 but has a corner there, so it is not differentiable.

Practice

  1. Is f(x) = 1/(x − 2) continuous at x = 2?
    Show answer

    No. f(2) does not exist, and there is a vertical asymptote (infinite discontinuity).

  2. Is f(x) = |x − 3| differentiable at 3?
    Show answer

    No. It is continuous, but has a corner at x = 3.

  3. Find the slope of y = x² at x = 3.
    Show answer

    y′ = 2x, so the slope is 6.
