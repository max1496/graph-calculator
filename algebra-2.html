Algebra 2 builds on Algebra 1 with polynomials of higher degree, their roots, and theorems that tell you what a graph must do. This page collects the interactive tools on this site that go with Algebra 2, in a sensible order to study them.

How to study this unit

  • Start with polynomial from roots. Seeing how roots and multiplicity shape a graph makes the rest of the unit easier.
  • Use Descartes' Rule of Signs and the Rational Root Theorem together. Descartes tells you how many positive and negative roots to expect, and the Rational Root Theorem gives the list to test.
  • Use the Intermediate Value Theorem to show that a root exists even when you cannot solve the equation exactly.
  • Always check your answers on a graph. A root you found should appear where the curve meets the x-axis.

Key formulas and theorems

Factor form
f(x) = a(x − r1)(x − r2)…(x − rn)
Remainder theorem
f(c) is the remainder when f(x) is divided by (x − c)
Factor theorem
(x − c) is a factor of f(x) exactly when f(c) = 0
Rational Root Theorem
possible roots = ±(factor of a0) / (factor of an)
Descartes' Rule of Signs
positive roots = sign changes in f(x), or fewer by an even number
Intermediate Value Theorem
f continuous, k between f(a) and f(b) ⇒ f(c) = k for some c in [a, b]
Fundamental Theorem of Algebra
a polynomial of degree n has exactly n complex roots, counted with multiplicity

For a polynomial, a root where the factor appears an odd number of times is crossed by the graph, and a root with an even multiplicity is only touched.
