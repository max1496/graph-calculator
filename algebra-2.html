Algebra 2

In short: A guided path from polynomial roots to rational functions. Six short steps.

Algebra 2 is a detective story: given a polynomial, where does it hit zero, and what does the graph do in between? The theorems here let you answer before you draw anything.

Your path

Go in order, or jump to what you need. Each step is short.

  1. Function transformationswarm-up · toolSlide, stretch and flip any graph. Everything later builds on this.
  2. Polynomial from rootstoolPick the roots, get the polynomial. See how multiplicity bends the graph.
  3. Rational Root TheoremtoolA short list of the only fractions that can be roots.
  4. Descartes' Rule of SignstoolCount sign changes to know how many positive and negative roots to hunt for.
  5. Rational functionstoolHoles, asymptotes and intercepts: factoring pays off here.
  6. Ellipses and hyperbolaslessonCircles, ellipses, hyperbolas and parabolas with foci and asymptotes.

The big ideas

Roots are where the graph meets the x-axis. Every polynomial of degree n has exactly n roots (some may be complex).
Multiplicity tells the graph what to do at a root: odd = cross, even = touch and turn.
Rational functions are fractions of polynomials. The denominator decides the holes and the vertical asymptotes.

Study tips

  • Do the steps in order the first time. Factoring is the skill that connects them.
  • Before you press a button, guess the graph. Then check. Guessing is how it sticks.
  • Stuck on a word? Use the search button at the bottom left, or read domain and range again.
  • Always check an answer on a graph. A root should sit where the curve meets the x-axis.
  • Need practice? Problems and a mistake notebook are waiting.

Cheat sheet

Factor formf(x) = a(x − r1)(x − r2)…
Remainder theoremf(c) is the remainder when dividing by (x − c)
Factor theorem(x − c) is a factor exactly when f(c) = 0
Rational Rootpossible roots = ±(factor of constant) / (factor of leading coefficient)
Descartespositive roots = sign changes in f(x), or fewer by an even number
Intermediate Valuef continuous and k between f(a), f(b) ⇒ f(c) = k for some c
Asymptotesvertical: zeros of the denominator left after cancelling. Horizontal: compare degrees
Fundamental Theoremdegree n ⇒ exactly n complex roots (with multiplicity)

Books to study from

A textbook gives you far more practice problems than any website. These are good choices for Algebra 2.

  • Glencoe Algebra 2 (McGraw-Hill). Well explained, with many types of problems, including challenge questions. The follow-up to Glencoe Algebra 1.
  • Big Ideas Math Algebra 2. Step-by-step examples and plenty of review. Used in many school districts.
  • Art of Problem Solving: Intermediate Algebra. A challenging book for strong students. Covers polynomials, functions and more in depth.
  • Khan Academy Algebra 2 (free, online). Short videos and practice questions on every topic in this unit.

Editions change often, so check that the book matches your course or teacher.
