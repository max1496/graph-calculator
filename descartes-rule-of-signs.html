What is Descartes' Rule of Signs?

Descartes' Rule of Signs tells you how many positive and negative real roots a polynomial can have without solving it. Write the polynomial in standard form and look only at the signs of the coefficients.

  • Positive roots: count the sign changes in f(x) from one term to the next, ignoring zero coefficients. The number of positive real roots is equal to that count, or less than it by an even number (2, 4, …).
  • Negative roots: do the same for f(−x). To get it, change the sign of every odd-power term, then count the sign changes.
  • Roots are counted with multiplicity, so a double root counts as two.

Worked example

Let f(x) = x³ − 3x² − 4x + 12. The signs of the coefficients are + − − +, which has 2 sign changes (from + to −, and from − to +). So there are 2 or 0 positive roots.

Now f(−x) = −x³ − 3x² + 4x + 12. The signs are − − + +, which has 1 sign change, so there is exactly 1 negative root. The actual roots are 3, 2 and −2, which matches the case of 2 positive roots and 1 negative root.

How to use the result

  • Zero roots: if the constant term is 0, factor out x first. Each factor of x is one root at 0, and it is not counted as positive or negative.
  • Non-real roots: a polynomial of degree n has n roots in total. Whatever is left after the positive, negative and zero roots are non-real (complex), and they come in pairs.
  • It gives possibilities, not exact answers. To find the actual roots, combine it with the Rational Root Theorem, synthetic division or a graph.
