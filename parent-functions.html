Parent Functions and Their Graphs

In short: Every function family has a basic shape. Learn the shape, the domain and the range of each one, and you can sketch most graphs quickly.

Parent function = the simplest member of a family of functions.

Every other function in the family is the parent moved, stretched or flipped.

Below are the basic graphs from Algebra 1, Algebra 2 and Precalculus.

Cube root

y = ∛x
Domain
All real numbers
Range
All real numbers
  • Passes through the origin and is always increasing.
  • Very steep at (0, 0): the tangent line there is vertical, so it is not differentiable at x = 0.
  • Odd function: symmetric about the origin.
  • It is the inverse of y = x³.

Floor (Gauss bracket)

y = ⌊x⌋
Domain
All real numbers
Range
The integers
  • ⌊x⌋ = the greatest integer less than or equal to x (round down). ⌊3.7⌋ = 3 and ⌊−2.3⌋ = −3.
  • A step function: flat steps with a jump of 1 at every integer.
  • Filled dot = included, open dot = not included. The jumps are discontinuities, not asymptotes.
  • Neither even nor odd.

y = cos x
Domain
All real numbers
Range
[−1, 1]
  • The same wave as sine, shifted left by π/2.
  • Starts at its maximum (0, 1) (even function).
  • Zeros at π/2 plus any multiple of π.

Tangent

y = tan x
Domain
x ≠ π/2 + kπ
Range
All real numbers
  • Period π; repeating S-shaped pieces.
  • Vertical asymptotes at x = π/2 + kπ.
  • Zeros at every multiple of π; increasing on each piece.

Circle (not a function)

x² + y² = r²
Domain
[−r, r]
Range
[−r, r]
  • Not a function. A vertical line crosses it twice, so it fails the vertical line test.
  • It is two functions put together: y = √(r² − x²) (top half) and y = −√(r² − x²) (bottom half).
  • Symmetric about both axes and the origin.
  • The graph shown has r = 2.

Even and odd functions

  • Even: f(−x) = f(x). The graph is a mirror image across the y-axis.
  • Odd: f(−x) = −f(x). The graph looks the same after a 180° turn about the origin.
  • To test one, replace x with −x and see what happens.
  • (−x)² = x², so x² is even.
  • (−x)³ = −x³, so x³ is odd.

How to move and reshape a parent function

Any parent function f(x) can be changed into y = a · f(x − h) + k. Each letter does one job:

  • k moves the graph up (if positive) or down (if negative).
  • h moves the graph right (if positive) or left (if negative). Notice that x − h moves it the opposite way from what the sign suggests.
  • a stretches the graph vertically by |a|. If a is negative, the graph is also flipped over the x-axis.
  • Replacing x with −x flips the graph over the y-axis. Replacing x with bx squeezes it horizontally by 1/|b|.

Example: y = 2(x − 3)² + 1 is the quadratic parent moved right 3 and up 1, and stretched by 2. Its vertex is (3, 1). Try it in the graphing calculator.

Which one is it?

  • Has a corner or a point: absolute value (V-shape) or square root (starts at a point).
  • Has asymptotes: reciprocal, logarithm, tangent, or any rational function.
  • Repeats forever: sine, cosine or tangent.
  • Grows very fast: exponential. Logarithm is its mirror image across the line y = x.
