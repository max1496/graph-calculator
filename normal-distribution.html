Normal Distribution and Z-Scores

In short: Move μ and σ, shade the area you care about, and see the z-score and probability.

Find the value for a percentile

What is a normal distribution?

  • A bell-shaped, symmetric curve centered at the mean μ.
  • The standard deviation σ controls the width. Small σ: tall and narrow. Large σ: low and wide.
  • Mean = median = mode, all at the center.
  • The total area under the curve is 1. Area = probability.
  • Heights, test scores and measurement errors are often close to normal.

The z-score

z = (x − μ) / σ   tells you how many standard deviations x is from the mean.
  • z = 0: exactly at the mean.
  • z = 2: two standard deviations above the mean.
  • z = −1.5: one and a half standard deviations below the mean.
  • The standard normal distribution has μ = 0 and σ = 1. Every normal curve can be converted to it with z.
  • A z-table (or this tool) gives the area to the left of z.

Mean, median and standard deviation of your data

Type numbers separated by spaces or commas.

  • Mean = sum ÷ count.
  • Median = middle value when sorted.
  • Standard deviation = typical distance from the mean. Population uses ÷ n, a sample uses ÷ (n − 1).

Practice

  1. Scores have μ = 70 and σ = 10. Find the z-score of 85.
    Show answer

    z = (85 − 70)/10 = 1.5.

  2. Heights have μ = 170 cm and σ = 8 cm. About what share is between 162 and 178 cm?
    Show answer

    That is μ ± 1σ, so about 68%.

  3. IQ has μ = 100 and σ = 15. Find P(X ≥ 130).
    Show answer

    z = 2, so the area above is about 2.3%. Check it above.

  4. Which is relatively higher: 82 on a test with μ = 75, σ = 5, or 90 on a test with μ = 80, σ = 8?
    Show answer

    First: z = 1.4. Second: z = 1.25. The 82 is relatively higher.
