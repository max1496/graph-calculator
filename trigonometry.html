Trigonometry

In short: Trigonometry links angles to lengths. On the unit circle, the point at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ), and tan θ = sin θ / cos θ.

What is trigonometry?

Trigonometry starts with right triangles. For an acute angle θ, the three basic ratios are:

  • sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse
  • cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse
  • tan θ = opposite / adjacent

Many students remember this as SOH-CAH-TOA. The unit circle extends these ideas to every angle, including angles bigger than 90° and negative angles.

Degrees and radians

A full turn is 360° or 2π radians, so 180° = π radians. To convert degrees to radians, multiply by π/180. To convert radians to degrees, multiply by 180/π. For example, 60° = π/3 and 5π/6 = 150°.

Other angles use these values and the signs of each quadrant. In quadrant I all three are positive. In II only sine is positive, in III only tangent, and in IV only cosine.

Key identities

Pythagorean identity
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
Tangent
tan θ = sin θ / cos θ
Reciprocals
csc θ = 1/sin θ, sec θ = 1/cos θ, cot θ = 1/tan θ
Negative angle
sin(−θ) = −sin θ, cos(−θ) = cos θ
Double angle
sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ, cos 2θ = cos²θ − sin²θ
Sum formulas
sin(A + B) = sin A cos B + cos A sin B
cos(A + B) = cos A cos B − sin A sin B
Law of sines
a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C
Law of cosines
c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C

Trigonometric functions as graphs

Sine and cosine repeat every 2π and stay between −1 and 1. Tangent repeats every π and has vertical asymptotes where cos θ = 0. See all three side by side on the parent functions page, or change their amplitude, period and shift in the function transformations tool.
