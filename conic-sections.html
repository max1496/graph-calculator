Conic Sections: Ellipses, Hyperbolas, Circles and Parabolas

In short: A conic section is the curve you get when a plane cuts a cone. Choose a type, change the numbers, and see its equation, vertices, foci and more.

The four conic sections

Circle
(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². All points at distance r from the center.
Ellipse
(x − h)²/a² + (y − k)²/b² = 1. The sum of the distances to the two foci is constant.
Hyperbola
(x − h)²/a² − (y − k)²/b² = 1. The difference of the distances to the two foci is constant.
Parabola
(x − h)² = 4p(y − k). Every point is the same distance from the focus and the directrix.

Key facts

  • Ellipse: the foci are on the longer axis. c² = (larger)² − (smaller)². The eccentricity e = c / (half of the longer axis) is between 0 and 1. A circle is an ellipse with e = 0.
  • Hyperbola: c² = a² + b². The eccentricity is bigger than 1. The branches get close to the asymptotes but never touch them.
  • Parabola: the eccentricity is exactly 1. The vertex is halfway between the focus and the directrix.
