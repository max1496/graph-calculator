Polynomial from Roots Calculator

Enter the roots (zeros) separated by commas. Repeat a root to give it multiplicity, for example 2, 2, -1 means (x − 2)² (x + 1).

How to write a polynomial from its roots

  1. For each root r, write the factor (x − r). A root of −2 gives (x + 2), and a root of 3 gives (x − 3).
  2. Multiply the factors together. If a root is repeated, its factor is repeated too.
  3. Multiply by a leading coefficient a if you need a specific vertical stretch or a given point on the graph.
  4. Expand the product if you need the standard form axn + … + c.

Example

Roots −2 and 3 give (x + 2)(x − 3) = x² − x − 6. With a = 1 the y-intercept is −6. To make the graph pass through (0, 12) instead, use a = −2, because y(0) = a(0 + 2)(0 − 3) = −6a. Try it in the calculator above.

Roots and the shape of the graph

  • Odd multiplicity (1, 3, 5, …): the graph crosses the x-axis at the root. With multiplicity 1 it passes straight through, and with multiplicity 3 it flattens out as it crosses.
  • Even multiplicity (2, 4, …): the graph touches the x-axis and turns around.
  • End behavior depends on the degree (the number of roots counted with multiplicity) and the sign of a. An even degree with a > 0 rises on both sides. An odd degree with a > 0 falls on the left and rises on the right.
  • A polynomial of degree n has at most n − 1 turning points.

This tool works with real roots only. Roots may be whole numbers, decimals or fractions such as 1/2. To explore a polynomial you already have, type it into the graphing calculator and open Details of graph.
