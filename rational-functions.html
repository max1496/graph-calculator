Rational Function Calculator

In short: Type the top and bottom of a fraction of polynomials to find its asymptotes, holes and intercepts.

A rational function is a polynomial divided by a polynomial. Enter the coefficients of the numerator and the denominator from the highest power down to the constant term, separated by commas. Write 0 for a missing term. For (x² − 1) / (x² − x − 2) enter 1, 0, -1 and 1, -1, -2.

What is a rational function?

A rational function has the form f(x) = N(x) / D(x), where N and D are polynomials and D is not zero. Its graph can have breaks: vertical lines it approaches but never touches, and single missing points.

Vertical asymptotes and holes

First cancel any factor that appears in both the numerator and the denominator. Each cancelled factor x − a gives a hole at x = a, a single point the graph skips. Every zero of the denominator that is left after cancelling gives a vertical asymptote. Near a vertical asymptote the function grows without bound, either up or down.

If the factor x − a appears an odd number of times in the remaining denominator, the graph goes up on one side and down on the other. If it appears an even number of times, the graph goes the same way on both sides.

Horizontal and slant asymptotes

Compare the degree of the numerator (n) with the degree of the denominator (m) after cancelling.

  • n < m: the horizontal asymptote is y = 0.
  • n = m: the horizontal asymptote is y = (leading coefficient of N) / (leading coefficient of D).
  • n = m + 1: there is no horizontal asymptote, but there is a slant (oblique) asymptote. Divide N by D with polynomial long division. The quotient y = mx + b is the asymptote.
  • n > m + 1: there is no horizontal or slant asymptote. The graph goes to infinity like a polynomial of degree n − m.

Intercepts

The x-intercepts are the zeros of the numerator after cancelling. The y-intercept is f(0) = N(0) / D(0), which does not exist if the denominator is 0 at x = 0.

Worked example

f(x) = (x² − 1) / (x² − x − 2)
  1. Factor both parts: (x − 1)(x + 1) / ((x − 2)(x + 1))
  2. Cancel the common factor x + 1. That makes a hole at x = −1.
  3. The simplified function is (x − 1)/(x − 2). At x = −1 it equals 2/3, so the hole is the point (−1, 2/3).
  4. The denominator that is left gives a vertical asymptote at x = 2.
  5. Equal degrees, both leading coefficients 1: horizontal asymptote y = 1.
  6. Intercepts: x-intercept 1, y-intercept 1/2.

The calculator above shows all of this on the graph.

Good to know

  • A graph can cross a horizontal asymptote in the middle of the graph. The asymptote describes what happens far to the left and right.
  • A slant asymptote and a horizontal asymptote cannot both appear for the same rational function.
